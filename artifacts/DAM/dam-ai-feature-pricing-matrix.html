AI Features: Cost Drivers and Pricing Implications

A working matrix for Sales. It sets out every AI capability in or near the DAM, what we actually get billed on, when that cost is incurred, and how each one should be packaged commercially.

Owner: Product Audience: Sales, Product, Finance Status: Draft for review Costs: public list, August 2026. Mixed provenance, see below

Read this before the matrix

A list of AI features against a price will misprice deals, because the feature is not the cost driver. Four things are.

01
The unit we are billed on

Per image, per page, per minute of media, or per month of standing infrastructure. These are not interchangeable. One hour of video costs exactly the same to auto tag as six thousand images.

02
Whether the cost recurs

Tagging and OCR are paid once per ingest event and never again. The vector index behind natural language search is paid every month whether anyone searches or not.

03
Shared infrastructure

Natural language search, similarity search and duplicate detection ride the same embedding pipeline and the same index. Sold as three separate line items, we charge three times for one build and invite the client to buy only the cheapest.

04
Backfill at go live

Switching AI on for a client with an existing library is a one off cost that has nothing to do with their ongoing volume. It is the largest single AI number in most deals and it is currently unquoted.

Positioning note

Every DAM vendor rents the same underlying models. AI capability is not our differentiator and should not be sold as one. Our differentiator is that the enrichment lands inside the Unity workflow, on assets that already carry approver and rights lineage. Price AI as an enabler of that story, not as the story.

Other factors to consider

Six things that sit outside the matrix but change the number on a quote more than any single unit cost does.

1. Library backfill is a separate, quotable line

Turning AI on for a client who already has assets means processing the entire existing library once. That cost is driven by what they already hold, not by what they will upload. For an image heavy library it is small. For a media heavy one it is not: ten thousand hours of existing video costs roughly $60,000 to label and a further $14,400 to transcribe, before any margin. This must be quoted as AI Library Enablement, priced per asset and per media minute at the point of migration, and it must be optional. A client can reasonably choose to enrich forward only.

2. We are billed per ingest event, not per stored asset

Every new version re-triggers tagging, OCR and re-embedding. A client with 50,000 assets and heavy version churn costs more than a client with 200,000 static assets. Any allowance we write into a contract must be denominated in ingest events per year, not asset count. Quoting off library size will systematically undercharge the highest touch accounts.

3. Model upgrades force a full re-index at our cost

When we move to a newer embedding model, every vector in every client library has to be regenerated. That is a full backfill we absorb unless the contract says otherwise. We need a position on this before the first NLS contract is signed, not after.

4. Uncapped AI on an unlimited upload contract is unpriced exposure

Storage overage is already a familiar commercial mechanic. AI enrichment needs the same shape: an included allowance and a published overage rate. Without it, gross margin on an AI enabled account becomes a function of client upload behaviour we neither control nor forecast.

5. The client's own AI stack is a services conversation

Enterprise buyers with an existing Azure or Google commitment will ask us to run enrichment through their tenancy. That is achievable but it changes the deal from a licence with a usage component into a licence plus integration services, and it removes our volume tier benefit. Flag it early in qualification rather than at contract.

6. Facial recognition is a legal decision, not a pricing one

Cost is trivial. Biometric processing under GDPR is not. Bynder requires a signed DPA for this feature. We should not list it as available until Legal has a position, and Sales should treat any inbound request for it as an escalation.

Not one enterprise DAM publishes an AI price. Every figure below comes from vendor support documentation, module listings and third party procurement data, not from a rate card. There are four distinct models in the market and only one of them is dominant.

The pattern that matters most

AI features migrate from paid add on into the base subscription as they commoditise. Bynder launched face recognition inside the paid AI module and then folded it into existing subscriptions at no extra cost during 2024. Automated tagging and OCR are further along that curve than natural language search, and natural language search is further along than agents. Where a feature sits on that curve, not what it costs to run, should decide whether we charge for it.

Nobody sells AI as a price list

The consistent structural point across the category: no competitor prices AI feature by feature. They sell one bundle, or they include it in the tier. Bynder charges for automated tagging and OCR and has built a fast growing revenue line doing it, so charging is not itself a weak position. Unbundling is. A thirteen line price sheet reads as a vendor who has not decided what their AI is for.

The argument against including enrichment in base

It deserves stating, because both category leaders have decided the other way. Including enrichment permanently forfeits an expansion lever, sets an anchor that is very hard to raise at renewal, and ties gross margin to upload behaviour we do not control. Bynder's AI module is a material revenue line, not a giveaway. The counter is that we are the challenger: we need the evaluation to be about workflow lineage, not about whether our AI costs extra, and we have a base subscription to protect that they do not.

One commercial consequence

Because the entire category is quote only, any published price of ours becomes the only public reference point in a competitive deal, and procurement will use it. This document should stay internal. Customer facing collateral should state capability by tier and nothing else.

Recommended commercial model

Three bundles, not thirteen line items, plus one optional one off. Per feature pricing fails here because the infrastructure is shared: it lets a client buy the one feature that carries the standing cost and decline the two that are free to serve.

  • Visual similarity search
  • Duplicate detection
All three ride one index and all three are live today, so this is a module we can sell now rather than a roadmap promise. It is also where the standing monthly cost sits, so it must carry a fixed platform fee rather than usage pricing. Sell as one module or the economics break.

  • Video label detection
  • Audio and video transcription
  • Scene level search
The only genuinely expensive tier. Priced per media minute with an included allowance. Never bundle this with image enrichment: a single hour of video costs what six thousand images cost.
Plus one

AI Library Enablement. A one off professional services line covering backfill of the existing library, quoted per asset and per media minute at migration. Optional, so the client can choose to enrich forward only.

Open decisions

  • Allowance sizing. What annual ingest event allowance sits in the base package, and what is the overage rate. Needs Finance input on target gross margin.
  • What the running index actually costs. Not a decision any more, a measurement. The vector index is live and serving three features, so the standing cost is on the current bill. This is the floor price of the Discovery module and it can be known this week.
  • Whether Discovery is already given away. Six capabilities are live. If they are inside the first customer contract at no separate charge, the anchor is set and a paid Discovery module becomes a renewal conversation rather than a pricing decision. Needs checking against the signed scope before the model is agreed.
  • Re-index liability. Who pays when we upgrade the embedding model. Now urgent rather than theoretical, because live vectors exist.
  • Facial recognition availability. Sellable or not. Needs Legal.
  • Agentic roadmap. Every major competitor shipped agents between March 2025 and May 2026. We need an internal position before a prospect asks, even though nothing goes on the customer facing matrix.
