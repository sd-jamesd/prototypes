Panel width contract

Two surfaces, two allocation rules. The lightbox gives reclaimed width to the panel because its media box is already height-constrained. Edit gives it to the panel because the panel is the work.

Revised 15 Sep. Both panels are now a flat 400px under one shared token, the percentages having failed the resolution test in section 03. The step to 520 is deferred to Phase 3 and applies to edit only. Small screens are handled by narrowing the modal gutter rather than the panel.

02

The contract

Two tokens, not one. The surfaces have different jobs and different panel content, and a single shared band gets one of them right and the other wrong.

TokenValueApplies toWhy this number
--asset-panel-width 400px Both surfaces: lightbox panes and all seven edit destinations One value, two allocation rules. Same content family in both places, read-only in one and editable in the other, so the panel should not jump width when a user moves between them. Set by the Rights territory picker at 400, and by the audit rows coming to both surfaces.
--asset-panel-width-wide 520px above 2170px of surface Deferred to Phase 3, edit only Only earns its place alongside the two-column value list, which needs about 460px of panel to resolve a second column. Without that work the step buys longer sliders and roomier version rows, and nothing that changes what fits.
--lightbox-rail-width 56px Icon-only lightbox rail Icon-sized. Never scales, sits outside the clamp.
--edit-rail-width 88px Labelled stacked edit rail Contract value. The build measures around 77, which needs verifying as drift rather than capture scaling.
--lightbox-gutter 48px horizontal, 32px vertical. Horizontal drops to 24px below a 1400px viewport. Modal inset from the content area Fixed, not a percentage, so the modal does not become the page on a large monitor. Enough margin to still read as dismissible. The gutter is what flexes on small screens, not the panel: a content-sized element should not shrink because the window did.
--lightbox-max-width 2200px Modal outer width Past this the media box runs beyond 2:1 and starts letterboxing from the other direction.
--edit-canvas-min 640px Edit canvas Below this the panel overlays the canvas with a scrim instead of pushing it, so Adjustments never works in a letterbox.

Do not reuse Scale's --layout-main-width and --layout-panel-width (65 / 35). That is a page-level two-column pattern. Thirty-five per cent of the edit surface is 633px, well past the point where metadata rows stop improving. Both tokens here are net-new and should go to whoever owns Scale as evolution candidates rather than get added quietly.

03

Why both are flat

A proportional rule only does work in the range where it sits between its own bounds. Outside that range the bounds decide and the percentage is decoration. Here is where each rule is live, at the viewports people actually use.

ViewportEdit at 24%Resolves toLightbox at 26%Resolves to

The edit rule is pinned to a bound at every common resolution except 1920. For it to give 400 at a 1440 laptop it would need to be 29%, and for it to give 520 at 2560 it would need to be 21%. Both cannot be true, so what looks like a proportional rule is a two-value step wearing one.

The lightbox rule fails the same test, which is not what I expected it to do. It is live only at 1440, 1512 and 1680, across a span of 62px, and pinned at 1280 and at everything from 1920 up. Sixty-two pixels of variation is not a proportional system, it is noise around a number.

So both are flat, at the same value. That is where this started three revisions ago, before the different-jobs argument split them into two tokens. The allocation rules genuinely do differ, and that part stands: the lightbox gives horizontal slack to the panel and vertical slack to the media, edit gives slack to the canvas. But the width itself is set by content, and both surfaces carry the same content family. One number, two rules.

The small-screen case is handled by the gutter rather than the panel. At 1280 a flat 400 would leave the media at 672 against 727 today, a regression. Narrowing the horizontal gutter to 24 below a 1400 viewport puts it back to 720. The margin is the right thing to flex, because it is the only element on the surface that is not sized by what it holds.

04

Why the lightbox gain is vertical

The media box today is 1101 × 635, a ratio of 1.73. Anything squarer than that is height-constrained, and horizontal width lands as letterbox rather than a bigger asset. In a real DAM corpus that is nearly everything: 3:2 photography, 4:3 product shots, square social crops, portrait, logos. Only 16:9 and wider converts extra width into a larger image.

So the horizontal reclaim goes to the panel, which is the only element that can reliably use it, and the media gain comes from cutting the 135px of top margin and 87px of bottom margin. Dashed outline is today, solid fill is proposed, both at the capture viewport.

Everything squarer than 1.73 gains the same 25% in linear size and 56% in area, and all of it comes from the vertical axis.

05

Behaviour rules

  • The ceiling is a hard stop, not a starting pointWidening the surface does not raise it. When a panel genuinely outgrows its ceiling, it either goes two-column internally or escalates to a full surface behind a terminal navigation control, per the widget terminal control rule. That keeps the contract permanent instead of renegotiating it every time a panel gets richer.
  • Audit trail and version history are sections, not destinationsThey fold into Details the way file metadata did under SCR-174. Rail stops are for destinations, sections are for content. This is the rule that stops the futureproofing question from becoming a width question every six months.
  • Panel width holds constant across rail tabsIn edit, a panel that resizes on tab change reflows the canvas every time and breaks predictability. Size to the widest destination and let the narrow ones sit inside it.
  • Below the canvas floor the panel overlays instead of pushingTriggers around a 1184px viewport in edit. The lightbox equivalent falls out of SCR-210, since a drawer overlays by definition.
  • Rails never scaleThey are icon-sized and sit outside the clamp in both surfaces.
  • Attributes does not set the numberIt is a faceted checkbox list, not label and input pairs. Its longest real value is 152 px in a row that already has 209 px, so it has headroom today and gains only dead space from more. Rights sets the 400. Its height problem is answered by restoring the missing count pill on group headers, not by width.
06

Out of scope

  • The lightbox push-versus-overlay rule, SCR-210Narrowed from an earlier reading. SCR-210 changes which side the panel sits on and whether it overlays the media or compresses it. It does not change how wide the panel needs to be for its content, nor the modal gutters or max-width, so the width contract is not blocked by it. What is blocked is the allocation sentence: if the panel becomes an overlay drawer, the media box keeps the full modal width and the horizontal reclaim stops being a trade at all.
  • Drag-to-resizeThe right end state for edit, and not this cycle. Long-dwell surface with heterogeneous panel content is exactly the case where the user should decide and the decision should stick. Needs persisted per-user state, continuous canvas re-fit during drag, and a reset. Phase 3, with this token as the default and drag bounds of 360 to 640. No handle on the lightbox: short-dwell inspection surface whose job is to recede.
  • Panel content and rail destinationsUntouched by this. So is the counter and chevron move into the lightbox header.
