Design notes

This release: the four built modes (Resource, Capacity, Project, Breakdown) as an always-visible switch, the range as its own control, and a filter bar that holds everything saved with a view. Capacity stays its own mode. Built on Scale v1.9 tokens only. Notes 1, 2 and 4 describe the next-release one-timeline design, which lives in the Resource Planner: next release prototype.

  1. One timeline

    There is no separate Capacity screen. People, Projects and Workload pivot the same data. Bookings or Capacity decides whether bookings are drawn. The header and filters never change between them.

  2. Capacity hides the bookings

    Each person and role collapses to one line with a number on every working day. This is the view for capacity-driven clients with dozens of bookings per person, and for anyone who just wants to know the team is booked correctly. Shortcut C.

  3. Click any day to see what makes it up

    Every capacity cell opens what makes up that number: the bookings, their hours, and a way to change them in place or open the booking. Works in both Bookings and Capacity, so the old capacity dialog is always one click away. Role cells show who's free to take the unassigned work.

  4. Long ranges are capacity only

    Quarter (by week) and Year (by month) can't draw bookings, so they open in Capacity. Clicking a period offers to jump to those weeks with bookings.

  5. Busy rows roll up

    Past four lanes, a row shows three lanes of bookings and a stack with a +n count on each day for the rest. Click to expand. Keeps the 50-bookings-a-week client usable in Bookings view.

  6. Totals stay pinned

    All people and Unassigned work stay at the top in every view and range. They replace the sticky totals from the old Capacity mode. Totals come from the server, since rows load in chunks.

  7. Roles hold unassigned work

    Work booked to a role sits on the role row. Its cells show what's left once that work is assigned. Drag onto a person to assign it, or back onto a role to unassign it.

  8. Projects view pivots, it doesn't rebuild

    Each project shows hours planned per day, then its unassigned role work, then the people on it with their whole capacity, so you can see if someone on the project is overloaded elsewhere.

  9. Saved views keep everything

    A view saves filters plus People or Projects, Bookings or Capacity, range and every Display setting. Renamed from Save filter. Ships with system views; starring views as tabs is phase 2.

  10. Four tiers of settings

    Toolbar for what changes daily (plan by, show, range, dates). Display for how my screen looks. Advanced for set-once options. Admin tools for tenant settings like RAG thresholds, which the workload balancer reuses.

  11. Colour comes with a number, or a stronger bar

    Bars only drops the numbers in Bookings view; bars move to the darker state step and over days get a thicker bar. Capacity view always shows numbers.

  12. View only where you can't edit

    People managed by another office show a lock and can't be booked or dragged onto. Calendar busy blocks are read-only. Switch Viewing as to Admin to compare.

  13. Hover card sits above the bar

    It waits 350ms, is pinned to the bar instead of the cursor, and opens above it, so moving down to the next row is never blocked.

  14. Drag shows the consequence first

    Moving, resizing or drawing recalculates capacity live and names the days it would push someone over, before you let go. Resizing keeps hours a day (Alt keeps the total). Every change can be undone.

  15. Pencil, not tentative

    Matches the booking form and how agencies talk.

  16. Breakdown is the third pivot

    The built Workload Balancer (RR 623720), renamed Breakdown because Workload Balancer is Adobe Workfront's term: Unassigned Bookings, then Assigned Bookings by role, person, project and task, with day columns and a week total, or one column per week or month on long ranges. Type into any task row. Arrows, Enter, Tab, Esc and F2 work like the Unity task list, and every total above updates at once. Person, role and section cells use the same capacity pill as the Capacity view. A week or month edit warns before it flattens uneven days, a rejected value says why, and Assign user suggests the three people with the most room. Edits show up in Resource and Capacity too.
