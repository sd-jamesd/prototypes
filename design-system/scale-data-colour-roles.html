      The rules

      Six rules decide every colour on a chart. They take the colour decision away from whoever builds the chart, including AI-generated widgets.

      1. Charts draw from --chart-* onlyNo accent, primary, meaning (success, warning, error, info, discovery) or workflow-status token ever fills a mark. Those namespaces keep their jobs in the UI around the chart.
      2. A colour keeps one meaning on every screenOn a chart mark, Crimson always means "past the reference". It never labels a department, a client or a phase, so the categorical set excludes it. As a project colour it only appears as a named dot or tag. Red stays with status and never fills a mark.
      3. Pick the role from the chart's job firstComparison against a reference uses ink, deviation and reference. Magnitude uses sequential. Above or below a baseline uses diverging. Categorical is last, for identity only.
      4. Deviation needs a drawn referenceCrimson fills only what sits beyond a line, tick or band shown on the same chart. If the threshold is a tolerance, draw the tolerance.
      5. Judgement lives in words"Overspend", "at risk" and "+19%" belong in labels, finding cards and Badges, where status colour and an icon are allowed. The chart encodes; it doesn't alarm.
      6. Text never wears data colourValues, labels and legends use text tokens. A swatch beside the text carries identity. The only exception is a label inside a fill, which takes --chart-on-strong.

      Categorical: identity only

      Four slots in this fixed order, then a grey "Other", all from Scale's identity families, so nothing new is added. Colour follows the entity, never its rank, so filtering never repaints survivors. No slot shares a hue family with a colour that judges. Green, amber, orange and red already mean success, warning, progress and error in Unity, and Crimson is deviation, so none of them is a series colour. Scale lists chart series as an intended use of the identity families, so a slot can share a hex with a project colour; the form keeps them apart (a project colour is only ever a dot or tag beside its name). Every slot reaches 3:1 on the card in both modes. Seven clients is not a seven-slice donut: sort them in ink (ladder rung 1), or show the top four and fold the rest into Other.

      Rule for AI-generated widgets
      1. Count the series before choosing a chart.
      2. Four or fewer: categorical slots in fixed order. Three or fewer for scatter, maps, small multiples and line charts where lines cross.
      3. More than that: pick a rung from the ladder. The finding's subject becomes the rung 2 highlight.
      4. Never generate, interpolate or reuse a fifth hue. A chart that breaks the limit fails the check and doesn't ship.

      Stacked segment order

      Segments stack from the baseline in meaning order, with a 2px surface gap between fills and the reference tick on top. Under estimate is not a segment: the ink bar stops short of the tick.

      1. Within the reference: --chart-ink.
      2. Pencilled, forecast or planned on top of confirmed: --chart-ink-subtle.
      3. Past the reference: --chart-deviation, always last, always after a drawn tick or line.
      4. Part-to-whole with no reference (billable, non-billable, non-project): --chart-ink, --chart-reference, then the hatched track. Identity charts use --chart-cat-1…4 in slot order.
