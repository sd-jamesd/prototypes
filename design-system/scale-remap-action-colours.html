Action colour remap

Unity 8.0 · 39 legacy classes and 14 shipped options, both to 10 identity families · built against the live semantic tokens

Two migrations, one destination. The cv-* dictionary in form config and the light and dark options already implemented both resolve to the same ten families. Config stores a family name and nothing else; every family renders as a step 3 fill, step 8 border, step 11 icon and step 12 text.

Reserved by the semantic layer

Six families are the semantic tokens. An identity choice landing on one of these makes a workflow marker read as a system state.

Notes on the legacy dictionary

PointDetail
16 of 39 are aliasesThree names resolve to #d6409f, three to #f76b15, three to #007a3c, three to #7ce2fe, two each to four more values. Config has been choosing between options that render identically.
Nine land on IndigoBabyblue, blue, bluepearl, cyan, electricblue, gentleplum, lightbluepearl, tranquility and turquoise. Blue is Info and teal and cyan are Accent, so Indigo is the only cool family available. Nine legacy values collapsing to one is the single largest consolidation.
cv-turquoise-bg is the brand accent#0c818f is --colors-accent-accent-9 exactly. A workflow marker has been rendering in the brand colour, so it reads as an interactive control. It moves to Indigo.
Yellow has no homecv-yellow-bg and cv-lemonade-bg go to Lime. The Yellow family sits 8.4 from the Warning semantic, inside the reserved band.

Notes on the shipped options

PointDetail
Tone is goneLight and dark were two ways of rendering one family. Each shipped option now becomes one family and every family renders identically, so the seven Dark options change appearance from a solid fill to a tinted one.
Four consolidationsBoth Greens to Grass, both Blues to Indigo, both Greys to Gray, and Orange Dark and Yellow Dark to Brown. Each is forced by a reservation rather than chosen.
Ruby droppedIt was in an eleven-family draft. Against Crimson it measured 13.8 at step 11 and 10.0 at step 12, the two channels that actually distinguish a badge, so it could not be told apart. Red Dark goes to Crimson instead.
Ten is the ceiling, not a starting point. Six families are reserved by the semantic layer, seven more are neutrals sitting 2.2 to 4.4 from Gray, and everything else falls inside 20 of a semantic. The palette is exhausted. When the request comes for more options, the answer is that there is no eleventh colour to give, and further distinctions have to come from a second channel such as an icon or a label. That is a better answer anyway, since a second channel survives greyscale and colour-vision deficiency and a colour does not.
